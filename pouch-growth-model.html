Pouch Growth Model

Working draft · v1

Three routes to $100,000 a month in total revenue, built on Pouch's actual 2026 spend and order history. Every route is the same four inputs: spend, CAC, average order value, and orders per customer. Move the levers and the whole model moves with them.

The finding

$100K a month is not an acquisition problem.

At a $93 average order value, $100K means roughly 1,075 orders a month. August delivered 281. Buying that many first orders outright would cost more than the revenue they produce, so no spend plan alone reaches the target.

What closes the gap is how many times a customer comes back. That is the model's real lever, it compounds while spend does not, and it is the one Kuma does not control.

4.6 Break-even k

At the target, this is the lifetime orders per customer at which gross profit exactly covers all marketing, including the fixed $13,869. Pouch sits at roughly 2.2 today. Their own subscriber math claims 8. The entire question is what share of the base behaves like a subscriber.

That figure assumes a settled business. While the budget is climbing, the repeat base lags behind the spend that created it, so the real requirement is higher. Each route shows its own ramp-adjusted break-even underneath the k slider, and the gap between the two numbers is the price of growing fast.

What this draft does not yet do

  • CAC is unsettled. The $115 here is paid media over all new customers, per the September methodology. Pouch's own scorecard divides loaded marketing cost by paid conversions and reads about $150 for August. One definition has to be agreed before any output on this page is quotable.
  • Powder is not modeled separately. The Q1 launch changes AOV, CAC and k. Because the engine runs on those three inputs rather than on a product, powder is a new set of values rather than a rebuild. It needs a confirmed launch date and day-one inventory first.
  • Better retention only applies to customers acquired from here on. Everyone already on the books keeps today's behaviour, which is why all three routes start at the same place and separate over time. Reactivating lapsed customers is upside this model does not count.
  • k is one blended number here. The real structure is a subscriber cohort near 8 orders and a single-purchase cohort near 1.5, mixed by attach rate. Version two should split them so attach rate and hold rate become the visible levers instead of k.
  • Organic sits inside the CAC denominator, following Pouch's stated method. Worth noting their own working file puts paid at 75% of sales while Kuma has been working from roughly 45%, which materially changes the spend any target implies.
  • No seasonality and no competitive response. BFCM, powder category crowding and CPM inflation are all flat in this draft.
